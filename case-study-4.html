Where tranquility meets modern living

Sonder House is a modern residential property experience designed to showcase contemporary homes and apartments while turning property browsing into an aspirational lifestyle experience.

The website is not designed simply as a property listing platform.

Its primary role is to communicate three things immediately:

What the property looks like.

What kind of lifestyle it offers.

Why someone should explore it further.

The experience combines high-impact architectural imagery, minimal navigation, strong typography, property information, layouts, location details, and social proof to create a premium first impression.

Why Sonder House Exists

Buying or exploring a premium property is rarely based on specifications alone.

Potential buyers want to imagine:

  • What their home could look like
  • What the surrounding environment feels like
  • How peaceful the location is
  • How close the property is to the city
  • Whether the architecture matches their lifestyle
  • Whether the developer feels trustworthy

Traditional real-estate websites often focus heavily on listings, specifications, and functional information.

Sonder House takes a more emotional approach.

The website first sells the feeling of living there, and then gives users the information they need to explore the property.

The Business Problem

The core business challenge is turning a property into a compelling digital proposition.

A residential developer needs more than a beautiful building.

The website has to communicate:

Trust

Potential buyers need confidence that they are dealing with a credible property developer.

Differentiation

Modern residential properties can look similar when presented through standard listing layouts.

Emotional Value

A home is a lifestyle purchase. The website needs to communicate how the property feels, not only what it contains.

Information Accessibility

Once users become interested, they need easy access to:

  • Apartment information
  • Layouts
  • Gallery
  • Location
  • Contact information

Conversion

The final goal is to move visitors from:

“This looks beautiful.”

to:

“I want to explore this property.”

Primary Audience

Modern Home Buyers

People looking for contemporary private homes or premium residential properties.

They are likely to care about:

  • Architecture
  • Privacy
  • Location
  • Nature
  • Interior/exterior quality
  • Lifestyle
  • Long-term property value

Secondary Audience

Property Investors

Users who may evaluate the property based on:

  • Location
  • Development quality
  • Market positioning
  • Property type
  • Developer credibility

Additional Audience

People researching premium residential developments before contacting a sales representative.

01 — Real Estate Websites Can Feel Generic

Many property websites present homes as a collection of specifications.

This makes it difficult for premium properties to establish a distinct identity.

02 — Buyers Need to Visualize the Lifestyle

A floor plan tells users the structure of a home.

It does not necessarily communicate what it feels like to live there.

03 — Information Can Become Fragmented

Property information may be distributed across:

  • Gallery
  • Layouts
  • Location
  • About
  • Contact

The navigation needs to make these destinations easy to find.

04 — Trust Must Be Established Quickly

Property purchases involve significant consideration.

The first screen needs to create confidence before asking the user to explore deeper.

05 — Too Much Information Can Reduce Premium Perception

A luxury-oriented property experience can lose its visual impact when the interface becomes crowded with excessive text and UI elements.

The design focused on five major goals:

1. Create a premium first impression

2. Sell the lifestyle before the specifications

3. Make architectural imagery the primary storytelling tool

4. Keep property exploration simple

5. Build trust through social proof and clear information architecture

01 — Lead With the Property

The hero section immediately places the house at the center of the experience.

Instead of starting with a traditional property listing, the interface uses a large architectural image as the primary visual.

This immediately answers the user's first question:

“What does this place look like?”

The building occupies most of the visual space, making the property itself the hero.

02 — Sell the Feeling Before the Details

The headline:

“Where Tranquility Meets Modern Living”

does not describe a technical property feature.

It describes an emotional benefit.

This is an intentional UX and marketing decision.

The website first communicates:

peace + modern architecture + lifestyle

and then provides the functional path:

View Layouts

This creates a natural transition from emotion to action.

03 — Minimal Navigation

The top navigation contains a small number of high-value destinations:

  • Gallery
  • About Us
  • Apartments
  • Contacts
  • Location

This is an effective information architecture for a property website because it mirrors the questions users naturally have.

Gallery

“What does it look like?”

About Us

“Who is behind it?”

Apartments

“What can I buy?”

Contacts

“How do I reach them?”

Location

“Where is it?”

The navigation therefore follows the user's discovery journey.

04 — Visual Storytelling First

The property image does most of the communication.

Instead of filling the hero with paragraphs, the design uses:

  • Large architectural photography
  • Oversized typography
  • Short supporting copy
  • One primary CTA
  • Social proof

This creates a strong visual hierarchy.

The user can understand the product within seconds.

The hero is the strongest part of the website.

It combines:

Brand

Typography as a Visual Element

One of the strongest design decisions is the use of oversized typography.

The words:

SONDER

and

HOUSE

become part of the architecture of the hero itself.

Rather than treating typography as supporting content, the design uses it as a visual layer over the property image.

This creates:

  • Strong brand recognition
  • Editorial character
  • Premium positioning
  • Visual depth
  • A distinctive first impression

The typography also creates a relationship between the digital brand and the architectural form.

CTA Strategy

The primary CTA is:

VIEW LAYOUTS

This is an intelligent choice for a property website.

A generic CTA such as:

Learn More

is vague.

“View Layouts” communicates exactly what the user will get after clicking.

The CTA therefore creates this journey:

See the property → Become interested → Explore the layout

Property Information Hierarchy

The hero does not attempt to show every property specification.

Instead, it provides a small amount of supporting information:

Private homes

Surrounded by nature

20 minutes from the city center

They communicate:

Privacy + Nature + Accessibility

without overwhelming the user.

Social Proof

The +300 clients card provides immediate social validation.

The card is visually separated from the main content and placed in the lower-right corner of the hero.

This placement is effective because it does not compete with the headline or CTA.

The small profile images humanize the statistic.

Instead of simply saying:

300+ reviews

the visual presence of people makes the claim feel more personal.

Location Positioning

The text:

20 minutes from the city center

is particularly important from a UX perspective.

Location is one of the most important considerations in residential property decisions.

Instead of forcing the user to visit the Location page immediately, the hero communicates one key location benefit upfront.

This answers a critical buyer question before the user even scrolls.

Brand Positioning

The top-right statement:

WE BUILD MODERN HOUSES IN UKRAINE

acts as a concise brand positioning statement.

It immediately clarifies:

What they do + where they operate.

The positioning statement provides the rational context.

Together:

Emotional message → What the brand actually does

Visual Design Strategy

The design language communicates modern architecture through restraint.

The visual system relies heavily on:

  • White typography
  • Soft blue/grey environmental tones
  • Architectural photography
  • Minimal UI
  • Large typography
  • Rounded CTA
  • Soft card surfaces
  • Generous spacing
  • Minimal navigation

This is an important principle:

When the product is visually strong, the interface should support it rather than compete with it.

Image-First Design

The house image is treated as a product asset rather than a background decoration.

The architecture communicates:

  • Scale
  • Material
  • Lighting
  • Landscaping
  • Privacy
  • Modernity
  • Premium quality

This reduces the amount of explanatory copy required.

The visual itself performs part of the sales communication.

Step 02 — Establish Desire

“Where Tranquility Meets Modern Living.”

Step 03 — Provide Context

Private homes + nature + proximity to city.

Step 04 — Establish Trust

+300 positive client reviews.

Step 05 — Provide Action

View Layouts.

This makes the hero more than an aesthetic landing screen.

It becomes the beginning of the sales journey.

What I Focused on as a Product Designer

The primary challenge was to balance luxury storytelling with practical usability.

I focused on:

Emotional First Impression

Creating an immediate sense of place and lifestyle.

Strong Visual Hierarchy

Ensuring the property image and main message dominate the screen.

Minimal Navigation

Giving users only the destinations they need.

Clear Conversion

Using a specific CTA that matches the user's likely next step.

Trust

Introducing social proof without interrupting the visual experience.

Information Balance

Providing enough context to create interest without turning the hero into a property specification sheet.

Design Outcome

The resulting experience positions Sonder House as more than a collection of properties.

It presents the development as a lifestyle proposition.

The user first sees the architecture.

Then they understand the lifestyle.

Then they receive practical information.

Finally, they are encouraged to explore the property layouts.

This creates a natural progression:

Emotion → Context → Trust → Exploration

Key UX Decisions

From Property Listing → Lifestyle Experience

Instead of immediately showing specifications, the website first communicates what living at the property could feel like.

From Information Density → Visual Confidence

The design intentionally leaves space around the content.

This gives the property imagery and architecture room to communicate quality.

From Generic CTA → Intent-Based CTA

“View Layouts” tells users exactly what they can do next.

This is more meaningful than a generic “Learn More” action.

From Claims → Social Proof

The +300 client card provides an early trust signal without requiring a dedicated testimonial section in the hero.

From Navigation → Discovery Journey

The navigation labels map naturally to the questions a potential buyer has:

See → Understand → Explore → Locate → Contact

Key Takeaways

Premium products need breathing room.

Luxury property experiences can lose their perceived value when the interface becomes unnecessarily dense.

Sell the outcome, not just the specification.

“Modern living” communicates an emotional benefit that a list of property features cannot.

Images can carry information.

Strong architectural photography can communicate quality, lifestyle, location, and design simultaneously.

CTA language should match user intent.

“View Layouts” provides a much clearer next step than a generic CTA.

Trust should appear early.

A property website benefits from establishing credibility before asking users to invest time in deeper exploration.

Good real-estate UX connects emotion with action.

The user should move naturally from:

“I like this.”

to:

“I want to see the layout.”

to:

“I want to know more.”

Case Study Page Structure for AI

Section 01 — Hero

Hero content:

Sonder House

Where tranquility meets modern living.

Supporting text:

“A premium residential property experience designed to turn modern architecture into a compelling lifestyle story.”

Project metadata can appear below the hero:

  • Product: Sonder House
  • Category: Real Estate / Property
  • Platform: Web
  • Focus: Property discovery & conversion

The house image should occupy most of the viewport.

Do not add unnecessary UI around it.

Section 02 — The Challenge

Use a large editorial headline:

“How do you sell a home before someone steps inside?”

Then explain the challenge of communicating architecture, lifestyle, trust, and property information through a digital experience.

Use a split layout:

Left: Challenge narrative

Right: Cropped hero image

Section 03 — The User

Create three audience cards:

Modern Home Buyers

Looking for contemporary living and privacy.

Property Investors

Evaluating location, quality, and development credibility.

Property Researchers

Exploring options before contacting a developer.

Keep the section visually minimal.

Section 04 — Design Strategy

Use a horizontal process:

Attract → Inspire → Inform → Build Trust → Convert

Each step gets one short explanation.

This section should visually communicate the entire UX strategy.

Section 05 — Hero Anatomy

Add numbered annotations:

01 — Brand identity

02 — Lifestyle headline

03 — Property photography

04 — Primary CTA

05 — Location benefit

06 — Social proof

07 — Developer positioning

This should be one of the main case-study sections.

Section 06 — Selling the Lifestyle

Use a large crop of the house and landscape.

Headline:

“The property is the story.”

Explain how the architectural image communicates modernity, privacy, nature, and premium living without requiring excessive copy.

Let the image dominate the section.

Section 07 — Information Architecture

Create a visual journey:

Gallery → Apartments → Location → About → Contact

Under each item, explain the user question it answers.

For example:

Gallery

“What does it look like?”

Apartments

“What can I choose?”

Location

“Where is it?”

About

“Who is behind it?”

Contact

“How do I move forward?”

Section 08 — Conversion Design

Large text:

“View Layouts”

Then explain why a specific CTA is more effective than a generic action.

Show the CTA alongside a crop of the hero.

Section 09 — Trust & Social Proof

Headline:

“Trust should appear before the conversation starts.”

Explain the role of social proof in reducing uncertainty around a high-consideration purchase.

Section 10 — Location & Context

Private homes surrounded by nature, 20 minutes from the city center.

Present this as a location-value section.

Section 11 — Visual Design

Show the visual principles:

  • Oversized typography
  • Architectural photography
  • Minimal navigation
  • Soft blue/grey atmosphere
  • White type
  • Rounded CTA
  • Minimal UI
  • Spacious composition

Do not create a generic design-system section if there are not enough screens to support it.

Focus on visual storytelling principles instead.

Section 12 — Final Experience

Create a large full-width composition using the original hero image.

Overlay three concise statements:

Modern architecture.

Quiet surroundings.

A simpler way to discover your next home.

This should feel like a premium editorial ending rather than another UI gallery.

Section 13 — Closing

End with:

“From architecture to aspiration — every element of the experience is designed to make the property feel worth exploring.”

Then show the View Layouts CTA again.
