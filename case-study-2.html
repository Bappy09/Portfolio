One workspace to keep teams moving

Momentum is a team productivity and workspace platform designed to bring projects, communication, documentation, tasks, meetings, performance metrics, and AI assistance into one connected workspace.

Modern teams often use separate tools for project management, communication, documentation, scheduling, analytics, and productivity. While each tool solves a specific problem, moving between them creates friction and makes it harder to understand the bigger picture.

Momentum takes a different approach.

Instead of making teams manage multiple disconnected systems, the platform brings the most important parts of daily work into a unified workspace.

The design focuses on one core idea:

Help teams understand what is happening, what needs attention, and what should happen next — from one place.

Why Momentum Exists

As teams grow, work becomes distributed across more tools, projects, conversations, documents, meetings, and deadlines.

A typical workday may require someone to:

  • Check project updates
  • Review documents
  • Follow up on messages
  • Track tasks
  • Attend meetings
  • Review KPIs
  • Check team availability
  • Schedule upcoming work
  • Search through previous information

The problem is not necessarily the lack of productivity tools.

The problem is that the tools are often disconnected.

Momentum was designed to reduce this fragmentation by creating a centralized workspace where teams can see their work, progress, workload, and communication in context.

The Business Problem

The core business problem is fragmented team productivity.

When important work is spread across multiple systems, organizations can face:

Context Switching

People constantly move between different applications to complete related tasks.

Information Fragmentation

Project updates may live in one place, documents in another, conversations somewhere else, and schedules in another system.

Limited Visibility

Managers and team members may struggle to quickly understand project progress, workload, and overall productivity.

Poor Prioritization

When everything is presented as a separate task or notification, it becomes difficult to understand what actually requires attention.

Manual Coordination

Meetings, tasks, project updates, and team availability often require repetitive coordination.

Momentum addresses these problems by creating a single operational layer for team productivity.

Primary Audience

Product & Project Teams

Teams responsible for planning, building, managing, and delivering projects.

They need to:

  • Track project progress
  • Organize tasks
  • Manage documentation
  • Coordinate meetings
  • Communicate with teammates
  • Monitor workload
  • Understand performance

Secondary Audience

Team Leads & Managers

Managers need a higher-level view of:

  • Team workload
  • Project progress
  • KPIs
  • Upcoming work
  • Meetings
  • Team availability
  • Potential bottlenecks

Additional Users

Too Many Tools

Teams often depend on several applications for different parts of their workflow.

Too Much Information

Large amounts of project information can make it difficult to identify what matters now.

Lack of Visibility

A task list alone does not provide enough context about team capacity, progress, or project health.

Scattered Communication

Important messages and project information can become separated from the actual work.

Difficult Workload Management

Knowing what everyone is working on is different from understanding whether the team is overloaded or underutilized.

Repetitive Work

Routine coordination and information retrieval can consume time that could otherwise be spent on productive work.

The product experience was designed around five major goals:

1. Create a single source of truth for team work

2. Reduce cognitive load

3. Make productivity data understandable at a glance

4. Connect daily tasks with broader team context

5. Introduce AI assistance without making the interface feel overwhelming

01 — Dashboard as the Command Center

The dashboard is the heart of the experience.

Instead of showing a generic collection of widgets, the interface creates a snapshot of the user's current workspace.

The dashboard brings together:

  • Updates
  • Iterations
  • KPI
  • Meetings
  • Completion
  • Weekly workload
  • Calendar
  • Important project activity

This allows users to answer several questions immediately:

What is happening?

How is the team performing?

What needs attention?

What is scheduled next?

How busy is the team?

02 — Information Hierarchy

The dashboard uses different levels of visual importance.

Large metrics communicate important numbers.

Medium-sized cards provide supporting information.

Charts and visual patterns communicate trends.

The calendar provides time-based context.

This creates a hierarchy where users can scan the page rather than reading every element.

The design is particularly effective because not every piece of information competes for attention.

03 — Modular Workspace

The product is structured as a modular workspace.

The sidebar separates functionality into clear groups:

General

  • Dashboard
  • Templates
  • Products
  • Docs
  • Messages

More

  • To-do lists
  • AI Assistants

Interactions

  • Team members and collaborators

This creates a predictable navigation model while allowing the platform to expand with additional functionality.

04 — Contextual Productivity

A major strength of the design is that productivity is not represented only through tasks.

The interface connects multiple dimensions of work:

Work → People → Time → Performance → Capacity

For example, the weekly workload visualization shows how busy different team members are across a period of time.

The KPI card shows performance.

The calendar provides scheduling context.

Meetings show time consumption.

Together, these provide a more complete picture of productivity.

05 — AI as Part of the Workspace

The inclusion of AI Assistants directly inside the primary navigation is an important product decision.

Instead of treating AI as a separate product, the design positions it as another layer of the workspace.

This suggests a workflow where AI can potentially assist with:

  • Information retrieval
  • Project analysis
  • Task support
  • Documentation
  • Communication
  • Planning
  • Productivity workflows

The key UX principle is:

AI should support the existing workflow rather than force users into a separate AI experience.

Dashboard Design

The dashboard is structured around a three-part visual composition.

Left — Workspace Navigation

The sidebar provides access to the main product areas.

Center — Productivity Overview

The central area contains the most important workspace information:

  • Updates
  • Product/project progress
  • Iterations
  • KPI
  • Meetings
  • Completion

Right — Time & Schedule

The calendar provides immediate visibility into upcoming work and appointments.

This structure follows a natural information hierarchy:

Navigate → Understand → Act → Schedule

Productivity Metrics

The dashboard introduces several high-level metrics.

Updates

Shows the volume of project updates.

Iterations

Provides visibility into ongoing work cycles.

KPI

Provides a high-level performance indicator.

Meetings

Shows the amount of time being spent in meetings.

Finished

Communicates overall completion.

These metrics allow users to quickly understand the current state of their workspace without opening individual projects.

Weekly Workload Visualization

The weekly workload section is one of the strongest UX decisions in the interface.

Instead of showing workload as a simple percentage, the design visualizes team capacity across multiple days.

Different levels communicate:

  • Low
  • Medium
  • High
  • Fully Occupied

This makes workload patterns easier to recognize visually.

A manager can potentially identify:

  • Overloaded periods
  • Available capacity
  • Busy team members
  • Potential scheduling conflicts

The visualization therefore turns raw workload information into something that can support planning decisions.

Calendar Integration

The calendar is integrated directly into the dashboard rather than isolated inside a separate scheduling section.

This is important because time is a major part of productivity.

The calendar shows:

  • Current date
  • Upcoming events
  • Project-related activities
  • Participants
  • Scheduled times
  • Quick appointment creation

The "Make an appointment" CTA provides a direct action from the overview screen.

This reduces the number of steps required to move from seeing a schedule to acting on it.

Team Collaboration

Momentum also treats collaboration as part of the workspace rather than a separate communication layer.

The sidebar provides visibility into people and interactions.

This creates a relationship between:

People → Projects → Communication → Work

Users can understand not only what is happening, but also who is involved.

Visual Design Strategy

The interface uses a soft, spacious visual system with strong contrast for important actions.

The overall aesthetic combines:

  • Large rounded cards
  • Generous whitespace
  • Soft surfaces
  • High-contrast typography
  • Black/white interface elements
  • Bright orange as the primary action color
  • Subtle gradients
  • Abstract visual artwork
  • Compact data visualization

The orange brand color is particularly effective for actions and emphasis.

Instead of using orange everywhere, the interface reserves it for:

  • Primary CTAs
  • Active states
  • Important highlights
  • Data emphasis
  • Brand moments

This keeps the interface visually controlled.

Hero Experience

The central hero card introduces the product's core proposition:

“Maximize human productivity.”

This is supported by an abstract orange visual that creates a strong visual focal point.

The hero is not simply decorative.

It communicates the product's positioning while breaking up an otherwise information-heavy dashboard.

This creates an important balance:

Functional data + emotional brand expression

Component Strategy

The product uses a consistent card-based system.

Cards are used for:

  • Metrics
  • Updates
  • Calendar
  • Workload
  • Meetings
  • KPI
  • Completion
  • Hero messaging

This consistency makes the interface easier to scan and creates a scalable component system.

Each card follows a familiar structure:

Context → Value → Supporting information → Action

For example:

KPI → 3.78 → change from previous weeks → View all

This predictable pattern improves comprehension.

Responsive Product Thinking

Although the provided showcase primarily demonstrates the desktop experience, the layout establishes a component system that can be adapted to smaller screens.

The desktop dashboard is intentionally modular.

Each major section can become a responsive block:

  • Sidebar → mobile navigation
  • Metric grid → stacked cards
  • Workload visualization → horizontally scrollable chart
  • Calendar → focused schedule view
  • Hero → simplified mobile feature card

This modularity makes the product more scalable across devices.

What I Focused on as a Product Designer

The design challenge was not simply to create another project-management dashboard.

The focus was to answer a bigger question:

How can a workspace help people understand their entire work environment without overwhelming them?

The design therefore focused on:

Information Hierarchy

Showing the right information at the right level of importance.

Context

Connecting metrics with people, projects, time, and workload.

Scanability

Allowing users to understand the dashboard within seconds.

Actionability

Making important actions immediately accessible.

Scalability

Creating reusable components that can support more workflows and features.

Design Outcome

The resulting interface transforms a complex collection of productivity functions into a unified workspace.

Instead of forcing users to navigate through separate sections to understand their work, the dashboard provides a high-level overview first and allows users to progressively explore deeper information.

The design creates a balance between:

Data and simplicity

Productivity and human focus

Density and whitespace

Automation and user control

Key UX Decisions

From Task Management → Work Management

The product does not stop at managing individual tasks.

It connects tasks with:

  • Team capacity
  • Meetings
  • KPIs
  • Project updates
  • Documentation
  • Communication

This gives users a broader understanding of how work is progressing.

From Data → Insight

Instead of simply presenting numbers, the interface visualizes patterns through:

  • Workload charts
  • Progress indicators
  • Trend percentages
  • Calendar context
  • Status states

The goal is to make information actionable.

From Separate Tools → Unified Workspace

The navigation combines traditionally separate functions:

Docs + Messages + Tasks + Projects + AI + Calendar + Analytics

This unified structure is the central product idea.

Key Takeaways

A dashboard should answer questions, not just display data.

Every major section should help users understand something about their current situation.

Productivity is multidimensional.

Tasks alone do not define productivity. Time, workload, meetings, collaboration, and outcomes all contribute to the picture.

Visual hierarchy reduces cognitive load.

When every element has the same importance, dashboards become difficult to use.

AI works best when it is contextual.

Integrating AI into an existing workspace can make assistance feel like part of the workflow rather than another destination.

Great productivity software should create momentum.

The best experience does not simply show users what has happened.

It helps them understand:

What is happening → What needs attention → What should happen next.
