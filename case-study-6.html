About Tenista

Tenista is a modern digital experience designed around the tennis lifestyle. The platform brings together tennis facilities, coaches, schedules, events, and court booking into one visually engaging experience. Problem Statement

  • Tennis players find it difficult to search and book professional coaches online.
  • Court reservation tools look transactional and fail to capture sports energy.
  • One-time booking platforms struggle to build recurring local club communities.

Proposed Solution

  • Created visual coach profiles with calendar booking systems.
  • Designed layout tour sections featuring high-res active court photography.
  • Integrated dynamic event cards (e.g. Summer Open) to drive engagement.

Game On. Play Hard.

Tenista is a modern digital experience designed around the tennis lifestyle.

The platform brings together tennis facilities, coaches, schedules, events, and court booking into one visually engaging experience.

Rather than presenting tennis as a simple booking service, Tenista positions the sport as a complete lifestyle and community.

The website allows users to move through a natural journey:

Discover → Explore → Find a Coach → Find a Court → Join an Event → Book

The design combines editorial sports photography, bold typography, structured content sections, coach profiles, event cards, facility information, partner brands, and strong booking CTAs to create an experience that feels both premium and energetic.

Why Tenista Exists

Finding a place to play tennis is only one part of the experience.

Players may also need to find:

  • A suitable court
  • A nearby facility
  • A coach
  • Training sessions
  • Tennis events
  • Community activities
  • Playing opportunities
  • Information about locations

A traditional sports website can easily become a directory of courts and schedules.

Tenista takes a broader approach.

The product is designed to help users discover the entire tennis experience, from learning and training to playing and participating in events.

The Business Problem

The main business challenge is turning a tennis service into a compelling digital destination.

A tennis platform needs to solve several problems simultaneously.

Discovery

Users need an easy way to discover facilities, coaches, events, and opportunities.

Trust

Users need confidence in the coaches, facilities, and overall quality of the service.

Conversion

The website needs to move users from browsing into an action such as booking a court, scheduling a session, or joining an event.

Differentiation

A standard booking website can feel functional and generic.

Tenista needs a distinctive identity that communicates the energy and lifestyle of tennis.

Information Density

The platform contains different types of information:

  • Coaches
  • Facilities
  • Events
  • Locations
  • Partners
  • Schedules

The challenge is presenting all of this without making the experience feel like a directory.

Primary Audience — Tennis Players

People who already play tennis and need convenient access to courts, coaches, and events.

They may be looking for:

  • A court
  • A coach
  • Training
  • Events
  • New places to play

Secondary Audience — Beginners

People interested in learning tennis.

They need an approachable way to discover:

  • Coaching
  • Facilities
  • Training opportunities
  • Beginner-friendly sessions

Competitive & Experienced Players

  • Find quality facilities
  • Follow events
  • Train with experienced coaches
  • Discover tournaments
  • Maintain their playing schedule

01 — Finding the Right Facility

Users need to understand what facilities are available and whether they fit their needs.

02 — Finding the Right Coach

Choosing a coach is highly personal.

Users want to see the coach's identity, experience, and specialization before making a decision.

03 — Discovering Events

Tennis players may be interested in tournaments, seasonal events, or special sessions, but these opportunities can be difficult to discover when they are buried inside a booking system.

04 — Too Much Functional Information

A purely functional booking interface can make tennis feel transactional.

The experience needs to preserve the excitement of the sport.

05 — Moving From Inspiration to Action

Beautiful tennis photography can create interest, but the interface must provide a clear next step.

The design focuses on five major objectives:

1. Create an energetic tennis-first brand experience

2. Make facilities and coaches easy to discover

3. Connect discovery directly with booking

4. Build trust through coaches, partners, and social proof

5. Balance editorial storytelling with functional information

Hero Visual Strategy

The tennis player is shown in motion.

This is an important visual choice.

A static portrait would communicate the sport.

An action image communicates the energy of the sport.

The motion blur around the player reinforces:

  • Speed
  • Movement

  • Athleticism
  • Performance

The visual therefore supports the headline “MOVE FASTER.”

This creates alignment between copy and imagery.

Brand Typography

The hero uses extremely large typography.

MOVE FASTER

is almost treated as a graphic object rather than ordinary text.

This gives the brand a strong editorial and sports-magazine quality.

Large typography also creates a visual contrast with the smaller supporting navigation and UI elements.

The result feels contemporary and premium.

Facility Discovery

Facility Cards

The facility section uses large photographic cards.

Each card communicates the environment visually.

This is particularly effective for tennis facilities because users care about the physical experience.

A photograph can communicate:

  • Court quality
  • Environment
  • Atmosphere
  • Lighting
  • Surroundings

much faster than a paragraph of text.

Coach & Schedule

The Coach And Schedule section introduces real people into the experience.

This is important because coaching is a trust-based service.

The coach cards provide:

  • Coach photograph
  • Name

  • Supporting information
  • Schedule-related information

This helps users move from:

“I want to improve my tennis.”

to:

“I can see who can help me.”

Human-Centered Design

The coach section makes the platform feel more human.

Instead of presenting coaching as an anonymous service, the interface introduces actual individuals.

This reduces the psychological distance between the platform and the user.

The player is not simply booking:

“Coaching — $XX”

They are choosing a person.

That makes the service feel more personal and trustworthy.

Event Discovery as Retention

Events serve an important product purpose.

Court booking may be an occasional activity.

Events can create recurring engagement.

A player may return because:

  • A tournament is coming
  • A seasonal event is available
  • A night session is happening
  • A community event is scheduled

This gives the platform a reason to become part of the user's regular tennis routine.

Partner Ecosystem

This reinforces the professional positioning of the platform.

Partner logos can communicate:

  • Industry connection
  • Credibility
  • Sports ecosystem
  • Product quality
  • Brand legitimacy

The logos are deliberately kept visually quiet so they support credibility without competing with the main content.

Brand Story / Video

The large tennis image with the:

This allows Tenista to communicate something that static interface elements cannot:

Video becomes an extension of the brand rather than merely a promotional asset.

Visual Design Strategy

The visual identity combines:

  • High-energy sports photography
  • Deep blue backgrounds
  • Bright lime/green accent
  • Large editorial typography
  • White space
  • Rounded image containers
  • Minimal UI
  • Sports imagery
  • Small micro-interactions
  • Strong CTA buttons

The bright lime accent is particularly useful because it creates immediate contrast against the blue and neutral backgrounds.

It is used selectively for:

  • CTAs
  • Active states
  • Navigation indicators
  • Important highlights

This keeps the accent color meaningful.

Blue + Lime Visual Language

The color relationship communicates two different qualities.

Deep Blue

Represents:

  • Performance
  • Confidence
  • Professionalism
  • Sports environment

Lime

Represents:

  • Energy
  • Movement
  • Action
  • Youthfulness

Together they create a strong tennis/sports identity without relying on generic green tennis-court imagery everywhere.

Image-Led Storytelling

Photography does most of the emotional communication.

The page repeatedly uses:

  • Players in motion
  • Coaches
  • Courts
  • Facilities
  • Events
  • Professional athletes

The product is effectively saying:

**This is not just a website for tennis.

This is where tennis happens.**

What I Focused on as a Product Designer

The central design challenge was to balance sports emotion with booking functionality.

I focused on:

Brand Experience

Making Tenista feel like a tennis brand rather than a generic booking platform.

Discovery

Helping users find facilities, coaches, and events.

Trust

Introducing people, social proof, and partner brands.

Conversion

Keeping booking actions visible throughout the journey.

Visual Storytelling

Using action photography and video to communicate the energy of tennis.

Scalability

Creating a structure that can support multiple facilities, coaches, events, and locations.

The final experience transforms a traditional tennis booking concept into a broader digital tennis ecosystem.

Users can:

Key UX Decisions

From Booking Website → Tennis Lifestyle

The product does not begin by asking:

“Which court do you want to book?”

It first creates desire and excitement around tennis.

From Facilities → Experiences

Facilities are presented through photography rather than technical specifications alone.

This helps users imagine the experience of playing there.

From Anonymous Service → Human Coaches

Coach cards introduce real people and make the coaching service more trustworthy.

From One-Time Booking → Community Engagement

Events create recurring reasons for users to return.

From Local Facility → Tennis Network

The “Tenista in every state” section expands the perceived scale of the product.

From CTA → Motivation

“Your Court is Waiting” is more emotionally engaging than a generic “Book Now.”

It connects the booking action with the user's desire to play.

Key Takeaways

Great sports UX should create emotion before asking for action.

Photography can be part of the UX.

Strong sports imagery helps users understand the product before they read the content.

Trust is especially important for coaching services.

Introducing real coaches makes the experience more human and credible.

Events can turn a service into a community.

Recurring events give users reasons to return beyond a single booking.

Booking should be available throughout the journey.

Users can become ready to book at different points, so relevant CTAs should appear naturally throughout the experience.

Brand and conversion should work together.

The strongest sports websites do not choose between beautiful branding and usability.

They use branding to create desire and UX to convert that desire into action.
