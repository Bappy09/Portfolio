Bringing live football closer to the fan

ScoreBall is a mobile sports experience designed to help football fans discover matches, follow live scores, and quickly access the information surrounding a game.

Instead of treating a match score as a single piece of information, the product expands the experience into a complete match journey:

Discover → Follow → Understand → Engage

Users can discover important matches, see live scores, open detailed match information, explore lineups, review statistics, follow events, and move between different areas of the sports experience from one mobile application.

The design challenge was to make a large amount of fast-changing sports information feel exciting without becoming overwhelming.

Why ScoreBall Exists

Football fans do not only want to know who won.

During a match, they may want to know:

  • What is the current score?
  • How much time is left?
  • Who scored?
  • Which players are starting?
  • What is the lineup?
  • What are the match statistics?
  • What competitions are currently active?
  • Which matches are happening now?
  • What matches are coming next?

Traditional score interfaces often prioritize the score and leave the rest of the experience fragmented.

ScoreBall brings these pieces together into a focused mobile experience where live information is available within a few taps.

The Business Problem

The core product challenge is information overload in live sports.

Sports fans have access to enormous amounts of information, but the challenge is presenting the right information at the right moment.

A sports application needs to balance two competing needs:

Speed

Users want to understand the current match state immediately.

Depth

Users also want access to detailed information when they decide to explore further.

If an app shows too little information, it feels incomplete.

If it shows everything at once, it becomes difficult to scan.

ScoreBall solves this through a layered information architecture.

Primary Audience — Football Fans

People who regularly follow football matches and want quick access to live information.

They may be:

  • Casual football viewers
  • Dedicated club supporters
  • League followers
  • Champions League fans
  • Fans following multiple matches simultaneously

Secondary Audience — Football Data Enthusiasts

Users who want more than the final score.

They are interested in:

  • Lineups
  • Match events
  • Player information
  • Statistics
  • Commentary
  • Match timing
  • Competition information

01 — Finding the Right Match

When many games are happening simultaneously, users need a fast way to identify the matches that matter to them.

02 — Live Information Changes Quickly

Scores, match times, goals, and events change continuously.

The interface needs to make the current state immediately visible.

03 — Too Much Match Data

A match can contain scores, goals, cards, players, formations, statistics, and commentary.

Presenting all of this simultaneously creates unnecessary cognitive load.

04 — Switching Between Contexts

Fans may want to move from the overall match score to lineups or statistics without losing the context of the game.

05 — Mobile Screen Constraints

A football application has a large amount of information but a relatively small screen.

The UX needs to prioritize information carefully.

The product experience was designed around five major goals:

1. Make live scores immediately understandable

2. Help users discover important matches quickly

3. Organize deep match information into clear layers

4. Create an engaging football-first visual identity

5. Make the experience feel fast, energetic, and mobile-native

01 — Match First

The match is always the primary object.

Rather than beginning with generic content, the home experience surfaces important football events and match cards.

The user immediately sees:

  • Teams
  • Competition
  • Score
  • Match status
  • Match time

This establishes context before presenting deeper information.

02 — Progressive Match Details

The match-detail screen demonstrates a strong progressive-disclosure approach.

At the top:

Competition → Teams → Score → Match time

Then:

Events → Lineups → Stats → Commentary

This creates layers of information.

Users who only want the score can stop at the top.

Users who want deeper analysis can continue into the tabs.

This is particularly important for sports UX because different users have different information needs.

03 — Content Prioritization

The interface establishes a clear priority hierarchy.

Level 1 — Match State

Score and live status.

Level 2 — Match Context

Teams, competition, and time.

Level 3 — Match Events

Goals and important moments.

Level 4 — Detailed Information

Lineups, statistics, and commentary.

This prevents secondary information from competing with the most important information.

04 — Sport-Based Discovery

The home screen includes a sport/category selector.

Football is presented as the primary selected category, while other sports are accessible through the same horizontal navigation pattern.

This creates an extensible structure.

The application can potentially support multiple sports without redesigning the entire information architecture.

Home Experience

The home screen acts as the user's live sports hub.

The top area includes:

  • Personalized greeting
  • User profile
  • Search
  • Sport selector

Below that, the Top Event section highlights an important live match.

The match card communicates the essential information immediately:

Chelsea — 3 : 3 — Manchester United

along with live timing and match status.

This makes the most important event visually dominant.

Match Discovery

Below the top event, the interface introduces competition-based content.

The UEFA Champions League section groups relevant matches under a recognizable competition context.

Each match card provides:

  • Team identity
  • Date
  • Kickoff time
  • Match status / context
  • Supporting match information

This allows users to browse upcoming or relevant games without opening every match individually.

Match Detail Experience

The match-detail screen is the core deep-dive experience.

The header establishes:

UEFA Champions League

followed by:

Aston Villa vs PSG

The score is placed prominently in the center.

The live indicator makes the current match state immediately visible.

This follows an important sports UX principle:

The user should never have to search for the score.

Match Events

Under the score, key events are shown with player names and timestamps.

For example:

  • Tielemans — 34'
  • McGinn — 55'
  • Konsa — 87'
  • Hakimi — 11'
  • Nuno Mendes — 27'

This allows fans to understand how the current score developed without opening a separate timeline.

The event information is kept compact so it does not compete with the score.

Lineup Experience

The Lineups tab transforms the match screen into a visual representation of the team formation.

Instead of using a traditional list, players are positioned on a football pitch.

This is a strong interaction decision because football formations are spatial by nature.

The user can understand:

  • Formation
  • Player positions
  • Starting lineup
  • Team structure

more intuitively than through a text-only list.

This is one of the strongest UX decisions in the showcased design.

Statistics

The match-detail navigation also includes a dedicated Stats section.

This creates a scalable structure for deeper match analysis.

Instead of putting every statistic on the primary match screen, the interface allows users to access statistics when they need them.

This keeps the default experience clean while preserving analytical depth.

Commentary

The Commentary tab provides another layer of match information.

This is useful for fans who want to follow the progression of a match in greater detail.

The tab-based architecture means:

Score → Events → Lineups → Stats → Commentary

can all exist inside the same match context without forcing the user into separate screens.

Visual Design Strategy

The visual system is strongly influenced by football's energy and atmosphere.

The design uses:

  • Deep green backgrounds
  • Neon green highlights
  • Dark surfaces
  • High-contrast white typography
  • Team logos
  • Player photography
  • Rounded cards
  • Soft gradients
  • Glowing active states

The dark interface creates an immersive sports environment.

The bright green accent acts as the primary interaction color and creates a clear visual connection to the football field.

Color as a Functional Element

The bright green is not simply a brand color.

It also communicates:

  • Active state
  • Live state
  • Primary action
  • Selected category
  • Important information

This creates consistency between branding and interaction feedback.

Typography & Information Hierarchy

Sports applications need strong numerical hierarchy.

The score must be visually stronger than supporting information.

The interface therefore gives visual prominence to:

3 : 2

while keeping:

36:45

and event information secondary.

Similarly, team names and competition titles provide context without competing with the live score.

This creates a clear hierarchy:

Score → Status → Teams → Events → Supporting Data

Onboarding Experience

The first screen introduces the product through a visually immersive football image.

The headline:

“Dive into the thrill of live football!”

immediately communicates the emotional proposition.

Supporting copy explains the value:

Live matches, highlights, and scores — all in one app.

The onboarding CTA:

Get Started

creates a direct path into the product.

The Skip option gives returning or experienced users control over the introduction.

Personalization

The home screen begins with a personalized greeting:

**Good Evening

John Williams**

This creates a more personal experience compared with an anonymous sports scoreboard.

The presence of:

  • Profile
  • Favourites
  • Search
  • Sport preferences

suggests a product designed around recurring use rather than one-time score checking.

Navigation Architecture

The bottom navigation includes:

  • Home
  • Events
  • Favourites
  • Stats
  • Profile

This creates five clear primary destinations.

The architecture separates:

Discovery → Events → Personalization → Analysis → Account

This keeps the primary navigation focused and avoids exposing every feature at the bottom level.

What I Focused on as a Product Designer

The biggest design challenge was not creating a visually attractive football app.

It was creating a system that could handle high-density, rapidly changing information without overwhelming the user.

I focused on:

Speed of comprehension

Users should understand the match state almost instantly.

Information hierarchy

The most important information receives the strongest visual emphasis.

Progressive disclosure

Detailed data is available without cluttering the primary screen.

Football-native interaction

The lineup screen uses a pitch rather than a generic player list.

Emotional engagement

Photography, dark surfaces, green highlights, and live indicators create the feeling of being inside the match experience.

Scalability

The navigation and content structure can support additional sports, competitions, matches, and data types.

Design Outcome

The resulting experience creates a balance between live entertainment and structured sports data.

The home screen is optimized for discovery.

The match screen is optimized for immediate understanding.

The tabs are optimized for deeper exploration.

The lineup view transforms structured player data into an intuitive visual formation.

Together, these experiences create a complete football-following journey inside one mobile application.

Key UX Decisions

From Scoreboard → Match Experience

The product goes beyond showing the final score.

A match becomes a complete information object containing:

Score + Events + Lineups + Stats + Commentary

From Data → Visual Understanding

The lineup experience demonstrates how sports data can be transformed into something users intuitively understand.

Instead of reading:

**Martínez

Digne

Mings

Konsa

Cash**

the user sees the players positioned on a football pitch.

From Information Overload → Progressive Exploration

Only the most important information appears first.

Users can then explore deeper layers when needed.

This keeps the interface clean without sacrificing functionality.

From Generic UI → Football Atmosphere

The visual system uses imagery, dark surfaces, green accents, team logos, and live indicators to create a strong emotional connection to the sport.

Key Takeaways

Live information needs instant hierarchy.

When information changes every second, users should never have to search for the current state.

Sports data should be visual whenever possible.

A football formation is easier to understand on a pitch than in a list.

Progressive disclosure is essential for high-density products.

Users should see the most important information first and access deeper details only when needed.

Emotion matters in sports products.

Football is not only about data. The interface should also communicate energy, excitement, and the feeling of being part of the match.

Good sports UX balances speed and depth.

The best experience serves both users who want a two-second score check and users who want to analyze the entire match.
