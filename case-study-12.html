Problem Statement

  • Car rental apps are cluttered, making vehicle discovery tedious.
  • Critical specifications like speed and range are buried in long lists.
  • Multi-page booking forms introduce friction and transaction delays.

Proposed Solution

  • Structured the home experience around search and direct brand filters.
  • Placed four critical specification blocks directly on the details screen.
  • Simplified checkout into a visual confirmation card and single continue button.

12. CaRide — Car Rental Mobile App

Find Your Perfect Ride. Book With Confidence.

CaRide is a modern car-rental mobile application designed to make discovering, comparing, and booking rental vehicles simple and intuitive.

The product focuses on the complete rental journey:

Discover → Filter → Compare → View Details → Enter Booking Information → Checkout

Instead of making users navigate through a complicated rental process, CaRide brings vehicle discovery, specifications, pricing, and booking into one streamlined mobile experience.

The showcased screens demonstrate three important stages of that journey:

Car Discovery

Car Details

Checkout

Why CaRide Exists

Renting a car can often involve too many decisions.

Users need to figure out:

Which car fits their needs?

Which brand do they prefer?

How much does it cost?

What is the car's range?

What features does it have?

Is the vehicle available?

How long do they want to rent it?

Where are they renting it?

How do they complete the booking?

Traditional rental experiences can make this process feel fragmented.

CaRide exists to make the experience more direct:

Find the right car, understand it quickly, and book it without unnecessary friction.

The Business Problem

01 — Too Many Vehicle Choices

Car-rental platforms can have a large inventory.

Showing every available vehicle equally can overwhelm users.

CaRide addresses this with:

Brand filters

Popular vehicles

Ratings

Pricing

Search

Category-based discovery

The interface helps users narrow the selection before they make a decision.

02 — Users Need Important Vehicle Information Quickly

A rental decision isn't based only on the appearance of a car.

Users may want to know:

Top speed

Estimated range

Battery level

Weather

Price

Rating

CaRide brings these attributes directly into the car-detail experience.

The result is a more informative product page without requiring the user to navigate through multiple sections.

03 — Rental Booking Can Feel Complicated

A booking process can become unnecessarily long when users have to repeatedly enter information or move between different screens.

CaRide simplifies the checkout experience into a clear sequence:

Selected Car → Driver Details → Rental Period → Continue

04 — Price Needs to Stay Visible

The design consistently surfaces the rental price.

For example:

$199 / Per Day

and:

$199 Trip Total

Keeping price visible throughout the journey helps users maintain a clear understanding of the financial commitment.

Why These Four Metrics?

The design avoids presenting a large technical specification list.

The user gets enough information to make an initial decision without being overwhelmed.

Performance Information

This can appeal to users who care about driving experience.

Range Information

303 mi

For an EV-oriented rental experience, this can be particularly important.

Battery Information

The:

85%

Instead of hiding battery information inside technical specifications, the design makes it visible.

Environmental Context

The:

25°C

This can help communicate that the rental experience considers the user's current environment.

It also creates an opportunity for the product to surface contextual recommendations in future iterations.

Price + Booking CTA

At the bottom of the screen:

$199 / Per Day

is paired directly with:

Book Rent

This is an important interaction decision.

The user doesn't have to scroll back to find the price or booking action.

The final decision point remains fixed near the bottom of the screen.

Favorite Action

The heart icon in the top-right corner allows users to save a vehicle.

This supports users who are still comparing options.

Instead of forcing an immediate booking decision, the product gives users a lightweight way to express interest.

Checkout UX Principle

The checkout screen follows a simple rule:

Show the user what they selected before asking them to continue.

This reduces accidental bookings and gives users confidence that the information is correct.

The restrained interface allows the vehicle photography and important data to remain the visual focus.

The Main Design Challenge

The central design challenge was:

How can a car-rental marketplace provide enough information for confident decisions without making the booking process feel complicated?

The solution was to distribute information across the journey.

Instead of putting everything on one screen:

Discovery

contains only what is needed to browse.

Details

contains what is needed to evaluate.

Checkout

contains what is needed to complete the transaction.

Progressive Disclosure

This is one of the strongest UX principles in the design.

Home

What cars are available?

Details

Is this car right for me?

Checkout

Am I ready to rent it?

Each screen answers a different question.

Design Outcome

The final experience turns car rental into a straightforward three-stage journey:

Discover

Find the right vehicle.

Evaluate

Understand whether it fits the user's needs.

Book

Complete the rental with clear pricing and timing.

The product therefore avoids trying to solve every rental problem on a single screen.

Instead, it gives each stage of the user's decision its own focused experience.

Key UX Decisions

From Inventory → Discovery

Instead of showing an overwhelming list of cars, the interface starts with:

Find Your Perfect Ride

and supports search, brands, and popular vehicles.

From Vehicle Image → Vehicle Decision

The car-detail page doesn't rely only on photography.

It adds:

Speed + Range + Battery + Weather + Price

so users can evaluate the vehicle.

From Hidden Price → Persistent Price

The rental price appears during both:

Car Details

and:

Checkout

This creates transparency.

From Long Checkout → Focused Checkout

The checkout only focuses on the information necessary to move forward.

From Generic Rental → Personalized Selection

The combination of:

Brand + Search + Favorites + Ratings

allows users to create their own path toward the right vehicle.

Key Takeaways

The best rental experience starts with discovery.

Users should quickly understand what is available.

Filters should reflect how people actually shop.

Brand selection is useful because users often begin with preferences.

Product details should answer decision-making questions.

The most useful information is not necessarily every specification—it is the information that helps users decide.

Price should remain visible.

Users shouldn't have to hunt for the total cost.

Checkout should confirm before it converts.

Showing the selected vehicle, location, price, and rental period builds confidence.

Visual products benefit from visual-first design.

The vehicle photography is treated as a primary part of the UX.

Progressive disclosure reduces cognitive load.

Each screen provides only the information necessary for that stage of the journey.
